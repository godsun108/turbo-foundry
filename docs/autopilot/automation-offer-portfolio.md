# Foundry AI Automation — Offer Portfolio and First Customer Motion

**Commercial status: pre-sales research and demonstrations, not live delivery or guaranteed revenue.** No subscription, deployment or client outreach is initiated by this document. The missed-call demo is synthetic; all other packages are concepts.

## Product portfolio

| Offer | Buyer | Business outcome to test | Current evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Missed-call lead recovery | Home services, repair | Faster response to eligible, consented missed callers | Offline demonstrator only |
| Appointment follow-up | Appointment-based businesses | Fewer manual confirmations/no-shows | Concept |
| Lead intake and routing | Service businesses | Faster routing of website leads | Concept |
| Review and feedback follow-up | Local service businesses | Consistent, policy-compliant feedback capture | Concept |
| Customer support triage | Teams answering repeat questions | Less time spent on common requests, reliable handoff | Concept |

**Selling rule:** Qualify every concept as proposed, not operational. Do not offer a live package, charge for it, or promise an implementation date until its scope, lawful processing, staffing, costs, security and delivery plan are approved. Consulting/discovery engagements would need their own clear terms and explicit authorization before collecting fees.

## One repeatable customer process

1. Select one vertical: begin with HVAC, plumbing and electrical businesses; test repair/detailing as a comparison cohort.
2. Interview prospects to measure lead loss, hourly staffing burden, software access, current consent, and what an improvement would be worth.
3. Choose *one* painful task and present a fictional-data demo or a storyboard, labeled according to readiness.
4. Write a one-page scoped proposal: inputs, outputs, responsible human, integration ownership, privacy constraints, performance metrics, support, exclusions, provider fees, implementation costs, cancellation and acceptance criteria.
5. Run internal offline validation and the required security, compliance and customer-specific integration checks. Never process real customer contact data without permission and safeguards.
6. Once deployment and billing are separately authorized, deliver a controlled pilot and report measured outcomes. Scale a product only after repeatable positive economics.

## Discovery scorecard

| Item | Green | Red |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real operational problem | Decision maker can quantify frequency, delay and cost | Hypothetical curiosity only |
| Buying interest | Budget holder agrees to a concrete next step | No owner/budget/urgency |
| Inputs/integrations | Authorized access and supported vendor interfaces | No permission to integrate |
| Consent and privacy | Approved permitted workflow with suppression/escalation | Unsolicited messaging or uncontrolled PII |
| Success metric | Measurable baseline, outcome and evaluation period | Vague promise of AI magic |
| Unit economics | Conservative customer value and positive *fully loaded* contribution | Estimated fees exceed achievable value |
| Support | Named person responsible for failures and requests | No human oversight |

## Pricing logic — research only

Calculate provider/platform expense, implementation labor, ongoing support, compliance, refunds and reserves **before** quoting. Ask prospects about willingness to pay, but never treat hypothetical bookings as earned income. Use `pilot_economics.py` for scenario estimates only; it does not include all costs by default. Avoid promising guaranteed growth or fully autonomous operation.

## Sales funnel instrumentation (manual until approved)

Record only privacy-respecting business-level information, with permission and minimal retention: contacted business, source, use case, discovery date, interested decision maker, problem severity, next agreed step, qualified/disqualified reason, proposal approved, pilot authorized, actual collected revenue. Keep personal/contact data out of source control.

**Stage definitions:** researched → interviewed → qualified → demoed → proposal requested → authorized pilot → paying → retained. Do not count proposals as sales or signed pilots as cash received.

## Near-term milestone

Secure **three genuine discovery interviews**, document what they actually need, select the strongest single workflow to deliver, and validate at least one willing-to-pay prospect. That is the path to first recurring revenue, rather than increasing code volume without market evidence.
