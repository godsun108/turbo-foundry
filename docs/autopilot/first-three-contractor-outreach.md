# Foundry — First Three Contractor Discovery Packages

**Status: outreach drafts for human review; not sent.** These are vertical-specific templates, not personalized claims about any particular business. All offers are in discovery. Only missed-call recovery has an **offline simulation**. No actual SMS, CRM integration, paying customer, demonstrated ROI, or live subscription exists.

## Package 1 — HVAC service companies

**Hypothesis to test:** Time-sensitive calls for no-cooling, no-heat, and repair appointments may be missed during busy service periods. We do not yet know the true rate, current recovery performance or consent process for any prospect.

**Initial email / DM draft**

Subject: Quick question about missed HVAC service calls

Hello,

I'm researching a follow-up automation for service contractors and wanted to ask how your team handles calls that come in while technicians or dispatchers are busy.

We're testing an offline demonstration of a missed-call workflow, including duplicate prevention and consent/opt-out checks. It is **not yet a live messaging service**.

Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about how inquiries are currently handled, where delays occur, and what a useful improvement would look like? No integrations or customer data would be needed.

Thank you,
Foundry Autopilot

**Discovery prompts:** Peak seasonal call volume, dispatch hours, after-hours protocol, current phone/CRM provider, how missed callers are currently followed up, opt-in evidence, dispatch staffing, average gross margin per completed job (only if the owner volunteers it).

**Demo brief:** Fictional cooling-repair caller → missed call → check approved number, business hours and permission → simulate one follow-up; repeat callback suppressed; opted-out and non-consenting callers rejected. **Zero messages actually sent.**

**Success metric candidate:** Median time to first *authorized* follow-up and confirmed appointment rate, with a pre-pilot baseline.

## Package 2 — Plumbing and drain businesses

**Hypothesis to test:** Emergency and routine service inquiries compete for staff attention; automated acknowledgment may help only when lawful, reliable and appropriately escalated.

**Initial email / DM draft**

Subject: How do you handle missed plumbing inquiries?

Hello,

I'm speaking with plumbing businesses about how incoming calls are managed during jobs, after hours and high-volume periods.

We're developing a permission-aware missed-call follow-up concept and have a fictional-data, offline demo that shows safeguards against duplicate or unwanted messages. It is **not connected to real phone numbers or sending texts**.

Could I ask a few questions about your current response process in a short 15-minute conversation? I'm looking for feedback on what would actually save time for a plumbing team—not trying to install anything during this meeting.

Thank you,
Foundry Autopilot

**Discovery prompts:** Emergency-versus-routine triage; dispatch escalation; current voicemail return times; business hours; permission capture; provider account ownership; how quote inquiries become booked jobs.

**Demo brief:** Fictional inbound drain-repair inquiry; show eligible-event determination, consent and duplicate suppression, and manual handoff as a proposed design. Do **not** imply the software detects real emergencies.

**Success metric candidate:** Share of eligible inquiries receiving authorized follow-up within a customer-defined interval and documented booked-job outcomes.

## Package 3 — Electrical contractors

**Hypothesis to test:** Quote and service inquiries might be delayed when field staff are unavailable; lead qualification and human routing could reduce administrative overhead.

**Initial email / DM draft**

Subject: Question about handling electrical service inquiries

Hello,

I'm researching ways small electrical contracting teams handle incoming service and estimate requests when everyone is busy.

Foundry currently has an offline simulation of a missed-call follow-up workflow. We're also exploring lead-intake and routing automation, but that second idea is **only a concept today**.

Would you be willing to share how your current process works in a brief conversation? I'd value feedback about your biggest bottleneck, the software you already use, and what would need to be true before considering a pilot.

Thank you,
Foundry Autopilot

**Discovery prompts:** Residential/commercial split; emergency escalation; estimate request handling; approval workflow; staff routing; CRM integrations; permission and retention policies.

**Demo brief:** Fictional appointment/estimate inquiry with safeguards simulated in the missed-call workflow. Show the *lead-routing idea as a storyboard only*; never claim a functioning production AI dispatcher.

**Success metric candidate:** Time between initial eligible inquiry and first documented human assignment, plus conversion to *confirmed* appointment.

## One common next step
