# Fine China — chorus melody proposal (DRAFT)

**Purpose:** a concrete first melodic direction for us to audition and approve. This is a composition sketch, not a reviewed SoulX score. Nothing here is marked approved, and it must not be exported as production-ready singing metadata until we review the phrasing, notes, syllable timing and pronunciation together.

## Locked project facts vs. proposal
- Project tempo: 126 BPM, 4/4.
- Canonical project key: A minor.
- The 120-bar arrangement is approximately 3:48.6 at 126 BPM.
- Proposed chorus harmony: | Am | F | C | G |, one chord per bar, repeated.
- Proposed vocal range: D4–A4 for the lead hook, with gang responses centered around E4–A4. This is a starting range, not a singer-specific requirement.

## Hook contour

Aim for a joyful, slightly cheeky lift: the lead phrase climbs through “fine”, then resolves downward on “China”; the response is short, rhythmic and emphatic.

| Bar | Harmony | Lead / response direction | Suggested pitch palette |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Am | “Don't save the…” conversational pickup | E4, G4, A4 |
| 2 | F | “…fine China” opens up, then resolves | A4, G4, E4, D4 |
| 3 | C | Gang response “EAT ON IT!” — punchy, clipped | E4, G4, A4 |
| 4 | G | Leave a beat of air; short ad-lib or instrumental answer | G4, E4, D4 |
| 5 | Am | “Pour the good wine” mirrors the first call | E4, G4, A4, G4 |
| 6 | F | Hold “wine” slightly longer; warm resolution | A4, G4, E4 |
| 7 | C | Gang response “DRINK ON IT!” — same rhythmic identity | E4, G4, A4 |
| 8 | G | Instrumental turnaround into the next lyric | D4, E4, G4 |

**Performance direction:** verses stay close-mic and conversational; chorus lead becomes melodic and confident; gang responses sound like a joyful group around the table, not a stadium crowd. Double the lead selectively, keep the response wider, and let the kick/bass own the downbeat.

## Proposed chorus continuation
For “Wear your Sunday shoes on a Tuesday night” and “If you got a little love, you got a reason to celebrate life”, keep the melody narrower and more speech-shaped so the words stay clear. Save the highest sustained note for “celebrate” or “life”, then drop back into the hook. The final two lines should build toward the repeated “Don't save the fine China — EAT ON IT!” payoff.
