# Production brief — Fine China (Eat On It)

## Target
A finished, release-quality, approximately 3:45–3:55 soulful bass-house / funky disco-house single. At 126 BPM in 4/4, the existing 120-bar arrangement runs approximately 3:48.6 before any final tail edits. Club, warehouse, car and weekend energy. Strong groove, memorable hook, playful wisdom rather than a lecture.

## Musical spec
- 126 BPM, 4/4, A minor (the canonical project key; use F-major color in the harmonic loop where useful).
- Tight four-on-the-floor kick; crisp clap/snare on 2 and 4; swung shuffled hats and hand percussion.
- Deep rubbery syncopated bass-house bass, sidechain pump, tasteful sub support.
- Chic disco rhythm guitar, warm Rhodes/clav stabs, bright string/brass accents, short vocal chops.
- Intro and verses leave space for a dry, close-mic spoken/soulful lead. Pre-choruses add lift. Chorus opens wide with melodic lead and layered gang shouts on “EAT ON IT!” / “DRINK ON IT!”.
- Bridge drops to bass pulse and intimate spoken delivery, then a full-energy final drop.
- Human-feeling timing, confident groove, playful swagger, warm celebratory finish.

## Approved vocal identity — hybrid D
The user approved a hybrid performance direction:
- **Verses and intro:** warm, deep, conversational spoken delivery with relaxed swagger, intimate and close-mic. Slight rasp is welcome, but diction stays clear.
- **Pre-choruses:** transition from speech into rhythmic sung phrases; build anticipation and melodic lift.
- **Chorus:** open into a big, soulful, expressive house-vocal melody. Confident, joyful and memorable, not over-sung.
- **Hook responses:** layered group/gang shouts on “EAT ON IT!”, “DRINK ON IT!”, “LIVE ON IT!” and “THIS IS IT!”; energetic and human, like friends around a table rather than a stadium crowd.
- **Bridge:** strip back to intimate spoken delivery over a bass pulse, then build into the final drop.
- **Production:** selective lead doubles, tight three-part harmony on chorus tails, wide gang responses, controlled slap/short plate, natural breaths. Keep lead intelligible and centered.
- Voice must be original and non-imitative. Do not clone or imitate a named artist or use an unlicensed reference voice.

## Arrangement map (126 BPM)
- 1–8 bars: spoken intro, filtered keys, vinyl/room texture (no fake crowd ambience).
- 9–24: verse 1, bass enters progressively.
- 25–32: pre-chorus lift, hats and rising chord voicing.
- 33–48: chorus / first drop, full groove.
- 49–64: verse 2, groove stays but instrumentation thins.
- 65–72: pre-chorus.
- 73–88: chorus / second drop, added counterline.
- 89–96: bridge, drums strip away; spoken vocal and bass pulse.
- 97–112: final drop, biggest hook, gang shouts and harmony stacks.
- 113–120: outro, spoken tag, DJ-friendly tail.

## Mix / master targets
- Punchy kick and bass with mono-compatible sub; avoid masking lead vocal.
- Vocal intelligibility first; controlled sibilance, natural dynamics, tasteful parallel compression and short plate/slap delay.
- Sidechain bass/music to kick without pumping the vocal unnaturally.
- Check mono, earbuds, phone speaker, car and club translation.
- Master for modern streaming/club crossover: no clipping, true-peak ceiling around -1 dBTP, integrated loudness chosen by musical dynamics (roughly -9 to -10 LUFS as a starting point, not a forced target). Deliver 24-bit WAV master plus 320 kbps MP3 preview and stems.
- Preserve headroom in premaster; report actual measured LUFS/true peak. Never claim a master was measured if it was not.
