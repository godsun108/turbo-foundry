# Sovereign Future Technologies Initiative (SFTI)

**Status:** Foundation / portfolio established  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Host:** Turbo Foundry  
**Mandate:** Research, design, simulate, prototype, test, preserve, and responsibly mature physical technologies inspired by science fiction and grounded in scientific evidence.

## Approved priorities

- **Ambition:** Full-spectrum. Include practical engineering, emerging science, and speculative frontier research.
- **Primary focus:** Physical technologies: instruments, machines, robotics, materials, energy, biological and ecological systems, fabrication, transportation, and infrastructure.
- **Development approach:** Hybrid. Reuse and improve proven open research and existing components while developing original, independently maintainable technology where it creates genuine value.

## Mission

Turn science-fiction concepts into a disciplined engineering pipeline. SFTI does not treat fictional depictions as evidence or promise impossible outcomes. Every concept is classified by evidence and maturity, every prototype has measurable acceptance criteria, and every claim is traceable to reproducible tests or credible sources.

## Operating doctrine

1. **Evidence before claims.** Distinguish demonstrated capability, validated research, plausible hypothesis, and speculative physics.
2. **Build the smallest useful demonstrator.** Prefer safe, inexpensive, measurable prototypes before complex systems.
3. **Reuse before reinventing.** Survey standards, open hardware, peer-reviewed work, patents, and existing ecosystem components.
4. **Own the critical path.** Preserve source, designs, test fixtures, calibration records, and build instructions so core capability is not trapped in an external provider.
5. **Measure honestly.** Publish baselines, uncertainty, failure modes, negative results, and limitations. Never tune tests to flatter results.
6. **Human authority for consequential actions.** AI may research, propose, simulate, and assist; it cannot independently authorize hazardous experiments, medical interventions, high-energy systems, or irreversible physical actions.
7. **Safety and legality are design requirements.** Complete hazard analysis and obtain qualified review before fabrication or testing involving high voltage, pressure, heat, lasers, chemicals, biological agents, vehicles, flight, or human subjects.
8. **Preserve and compound.** Version research, record provenance, retain reproducible evidence, and promote projects only after they pass gates.

## Portfolio structure

The portfolio spans twelve domains:

1. AI-embodied intelligence and human-machine systems
2. Robotics, autonomy, and exoskeletons
3. Scientific instruments, sensing, and environmental intelligence
4. Spatial computing, holography, and interfaces
5. Energy generation, conversion, storage, and microgrids
6. Advanced materials, metamaterials, and additive manufacturing
7. Biotechnology, biofabrication, and regenerative systems
8. Ecological engineering, water, food, and closed-loop habitats
9. Transportation, mobility, and propulsion
10. Computing hardware, photonics, neuromorphic and quantum systems
11. Space systems, habitats, and in-situ resource utilization
12. Medical, assistive, and protective technologies

Cross-cutting capabilities: systems engineering, simulation, metrology, reliability, cybersecurity, privacy, lifecycle assessment, supply-chain provenance, and manufacturability.

## Maturity labels

- **M0 — Concept:** Narrative idea; no technical evidence yet.
- **M1 — Research validated:** Credible literature and prior-art review completed; feasibility assumptions stated.
- **M2 — Bench demonstrator:** Subsystem demonstrated under controlled conditions.
- **M3 — Integrated prototype:** Multiple subsystems work together against defined acceptance tests.
- **M4 — Field validated:** Tested in representative environments with reliability and safety evidence.
- **M5 — Product candidate:** Manufacturability, maintenance, security, compliance, cost, and lifecycle reviewed.

A maturity label applies to a specific configuration and test envelope, not to a broad technology category.

## Project gates

1. **Intake:** User need, fictional inspiration, intended function, constraints, and success metric.
2. **Evidence review:** Scientific basis, prior art, standards, competing approaches, unknowns.
3. **Feasibility:** Physics, energy, materials, cost, supply chain, manufacturability, and risk.
4. **Design review:** Requirements, interfaces, architecture, failure analysis, safety controls.
5. **Prototype:** Smallest useful and safe test article.
6. **Verification:** Repeatable tests, calibrated instruments, acceptance criteria, and evidence.
7. **Field evaluation:** Environmental, durability, usability, and maintenance testing.
8. **Promotion:** Documented review before integration, deployment, or commercialization.

Projects can be paused, redirected, or retired when evidence does not support continued investment.

## Ecosystem integration

- **Turbo Foundry:** Portfolio governance, project scaffolds, experiment lifecycle, evidence standards, and release gates.
- **Sovereign-Core:** Provider-neutral interfaces for Genesis research assistance, provenance, identity, policy boundaries, and evaluations. Core remains a shared kernel, not a hardware product UI or deployment host.
- **Genesis:** Research synthesis, design assistance, simulation orchestration, and evidence-grounded engineering copilot. It may recommend; it does not self-grant authority.
- **Alfred:** User-facing operator for approved workflows, status, and human confirmation.
- **Sovereign Offline / Online:** Local-first and connected interfaces as appropriate; physical devices must remain safe if connectivity or AI is unavailable.
- **EarthChain:** Optional provenance, ownership, maintenance, and lifecycle attestations only where useful; no critical real-time safety control depends on ledger availability.

Integration is staged. No repository, service, device, or agent is considered connected merely because it appears in this architecture.

## First execution priorities

1. Establish this charter and machine-readable portfolio.
2. Start TRICORDER as the first hardware-adjacent flagship because sensor modules are modular, testable, and useful across agriculture, ecology, field science, and environmental monitoring.
3. Start SENTINEL as the first distributed subsystem, sharing sensor, power, calibration, and telemetry patterns with TRICORDER.
4. Build reusable experiment records, test plans, risk reviews, and bill-of-materials templates.
5. Map existing Genesis, Alfred, Sovereign, and EarthChain interfaces before implementing integrations.

## Definition of done for the foundation

- Portfolio and governance documented.
- Every initial program has a unique ID, scope, maturity label, and first demonstrator.
- Experiment and risk templates exist.
- No unsupported claim of physical capability or cross-repository integration.
- Work is maintained on a reviewable branch and proposed through a pull request.
